# Games and controls

## The adventures

**Tone Tower** is an untimed, 100-floor listening challenge. Five pixel-art districts increase the choices from 2 to 3 to 4 to 6. Each correct answer lights a floor; the explorer enters the door before the camera climbs into the next room. Replays, mistakes and replacement recordings stay on the same floor. Easy, Normal, Hard and Extreme start with 5, 4, 3 and 1 lives. Mistakes reveal a bright, check-marked correct door and a small dust creature behind your choice, with a recoiling explorer and a breaking heart. Continue gives a new word on the same floor. Practising missed sounds is offered after a failed run, rather than after each mistake. Recover a life at intermediate ten-floor milestones and refill at twenty-floor landings.

**Skyrail** is one continuous, 100-span typing journey. Spell the recording to build the bridge before the car reaches the gap. Easy, Normal, Hard and Extreme start with the same 5, 4, 3 and 1 lives as Tone Tower, independently of Spelling or Full Jyutping. Both variants start at 15 seconds; stage deadlines are 15, 13, 11, 9 and 7 seconds. Full Jyutping requires tone digits. Begin with single syllables, then highlighted syllables, then whole words. Wrong spelling is free. Timeout costs one life, makes the car fall, and waits for review; Continue brings a rescue and a new recording without awarding a span. Recovery matches Tone Tower.

The camera and repaired track persist between recordings. Five stretches grow from cliffside houses and hanging gardens into copper cranes, cloudline pavilions and monumental gateways. The car upgrades at twenty-span checkpoints. Lives float above the car and the countdown appears on a trackside signal; numeric progress appears at checkpoints. The authoritative countdown begins after audio finishes, and replay never resets it. Correct answers allow a smooth 900ms crossing before the next recording. A missed car coasts until its rear wheel has passed the unsupported edge, then falls and is met by a rescue lantern. District stations remain in world space after departure. The input and scene stay mounted, including through audio recovery.

Each challenge variant has independent records. Adventures keep their animated ending, final word, summary and retry/map actions in the same mounted game view. Victories open into a lantern-filled summit or moonlit final station; defeats finish with a recovery scene. There is no second results page or ending replay. Runs are not saved when closing or reloading the page.

## Practice and help

Practice is a tab inside each game’s existing settings panel. Tone Tower lets you select a tone pair or a larger tone set and 2, 3, 4 or 6 answer choices. Skyrail offers initials only, finals only or full spelling, with optional tone numbers, defaulting to whole words with a Single syllable only checkbox beneath Include tone numbers. Sessions default to 20 questions, counting incorrect submissions as well as correct ones, with 10, 50 and Unlimited also available. Practice stays at the selected difficulty and uses the starting game scene, with no timer or lives. There is no separate Workshop page or building. Practice results never affect game records.

The home page introduces Jyutping as initial + final + tone with the example f + u + 2 = fu2 and a playable Hear fu2 button. The two enlarged map destinations are balanced on either side of the scene. Two main mode cards introduce Tone Tower and Skyrail. Game settings, Start and the smaller practice action sit beside the compact map on desktop and above it on mobile. The shared settings frame reserves space for Skyrail’s extra controls, keeping its dimensions stable when switching games. The Jyutping guide (`guide/`) is a bookmarkable introduction to initials, finals and tones, with six playable tone references, real word examples and answer formats. About (`about/`) explains the games, recording sources and browser-local records. Page navigation supports browser back/forward and direct links. During a run, Jyutping guide and About open as dialogs over the paused game; closing them resumes the same run. The compact in-game guide is shared with the same reference recordings. Ambient animation and persistent in-scene endings are included; reduced-motion settings receive stationary alternatives.

The guide starts with an introduction, a brief explanation of why it uses Jyutping rather than Yale, and a worked syllable. It moves directly into initials, finals and tones, without a separate “How to use this guide” section. The initials lesson covers all 19 initials with inline audio and explanations sized to the sound: familiar initials stay brief, while j, ng, aspiration and gw/kw receive more detail. Aspiration and the paper test introduce the initial-pair rows, without a separate disclosure or large initials table. Audio labels match the actual recorded characters. A single m/ng row in Finals explains vowel-free nasal syllables and contrasts ng5 with initial ng in ngaa4. A same-speaker haam4 鹹 / ham4 含 recording pair demonstrates aa/a, and an optional finals question explains the more open, shorter i in ing/ik and u in ung/uk with inline si1 詩 / sing1 升 and fu1 夫 / fuk1 幅 recordings. Fuller pronunciation lessons are linked beside each topic. Three collapsed questions follow the six playable fu tone references. First, the nine-tone answer defines entering or checked syllables, explains the six non-stop-final plus three stop-final categories, compares high/middle/low pitch with playable fu1/fuk1, fu3/baat3 and fu6/sap6 examples, and gives the normal 1/3/6 pattern with a changed-tone exception. Second, the Mandarin answer distinguishes similar pitch shapes from historical character-reading relationships, compares tones 2 and 5 through inline fu2/fu5 recordings, and gives a compact Mandarin-to-Cantonese tone table. It explains the historical yin/yang split and voicing, with m/n examples and a non-exhaustive exception example. Michelle Fullwood’s linked tables extend the Mandarin-to-Cantonese comparisons and show how frequent the mappings are. Third, the everyday-speech answer explains common Hong Kong pronunciation variation and uses same-speaker 頭 tau4 / 斧頭 fu2 tau2 recordings to demonstrate how a character’s tone changes in a particular compound. Resources are labelled by teaching language, purpose and assumed knowledge, including TypeDuck’s Cantonese-narrated video. Game and practice links follow the references. The in-game reference shares the same tone examples and resource links. About describes the exercises, their role alongside pronunciation study, recording sources and local storage. See [guide research](JYUTPING-GUIDE-RESEARCH.md) for source notes.

## Controls

- World map: left/right or up/down arrows select Tone Tower and Skyrail; Enter starts the selected mode. Home/End select the first/last mode. Shortcuts respect form controls and open dialogs. Destination walking never delays Start. Tower’s first recording plays automatically after a two-second opening; later recordings keep the usual pace between floors.

- Tone Tower: choose a door by click/tap or 1–6. With two doors, use left/right. With three, up/down chooses the middle. Four doors form a compass: up, left, right, down. R or Space replays. The question is displayed on its own opaque panel; scene artwork stays mounted during review.
- Skyrail: type in one field; spaces between syllables are welcome. Complete correct answers advance automatically. Enter builds; R or Space replays even from the answer field (IME and modifier shortcuts are respected). Spaced answers are still accepted when pasted. Wrong partial typing is harmless.
- Enter continues after review or a landing. Escape pauses or resumes. Returning from a hidden tab requires Resume.
- Audio errors stop the clock and offer retry or an unscored replacement.

## World and focused gameplay

The main menu is a connected night map: a central pagoda, upturned teal eaves, floating lanterns, a workshop and a railway station above the clouds. The character walks between the destinations; mode numbers and advance disclosure of the final floor/span are omitted. Saved difficulty and answer variants are selected beside the entry action. Tower runs show the entrance first, then play the first recording automatically after two seconds. The interior frames the actual choices. Success opens a green doorway and the character enters it; mistakes reveal a dark passage and the character recoils. Higher choice counts use tiered interior platforms. Root Court has climbing vines, Rain Gallery has rainlit windows, Lantern Workshop has copper mechanisms, Sky Conservatory has tall plants, and the Observatory has telescopes and stars. Hearts and a current-stretch floor gauge belong to the room, with a full-height miniature tower with 100 windows and a floor / 100 label; completed windows light from the bottom while the complete climb remains visible. Every twenty floors, the explorer reaches an open-sky rest landing: missing hearts refill one at a time beside a lantern fountain, and Continue waits until you are ready. Defeat dims the room, settles the explorer beside a companion and releases the last lantern; the summary stays below that scene. Victory transitions directly into the lit pagoda, rising lanterns and slow constellation bursts. Feedback and audio replacement use a fixed shelf above the word; review Continue belongs to the lower edge of the scene. The canvas stays mounted through review, audio recovery and changes in door count.

The word, highlighted syllable and Replay are always on an opaque panel. Skyrail normally places the input before the scenery. With a phone keyboard open, a compact panoramic strip sits above the input, keeping the car, track and approaching gap visible without squeezing the artwork. The mobile layout adapts to the visual viewport and also supports short keyboard-height viewports, preserving the word, input, replay and deadline while compressing the railway. Skyrail feedback has a fixed slot above the word. Review Continue appears in the scene; district stops tuck away the word and input to give the station a readable breather composition. Reveal and the bottom Jyutping guide have been removed. Reduced motion settles animation immediately. There is one fixed palette and no theme selector.

Skyrail uses the internal challenge key `tide`. Progress supports only the current save format; older formats start fresh.
