# Jyutping guide: research and editorial notes

Updated 6 November 2026.

The guide introduces the notation before linking to fuller lessons. It offers two starting points based on Cantonese familiarity rather than a learner’s nationality or first-language label. English comparisons are explicitly identified; articulation instructions and recordings remain the reference for the actual sounds.

## Language experience

- [Flege and Bohn (2021), The Revised Speech Learning Model](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108886901.002) proposes that existing sound categories, perceived differences and the quantity and quality of input influence new sound-category learning. This supports a general account of why learners have different starting points, not a prediction of an individual’s difficulty.
- [Balas (2017), The influence of second language vowels on foreign language vowel perception](https://doi.org/10.3765/plsa.v2i0.4085) studied Dutch vowel categorisation by Polish learners with different second-language experience. Experience with rounded front vowels affected perception. This is evidence about prior language experience beyond a first-language label; it is not a Cantonese study or a basis for ranking language families.
- [Mok, Leung, Lan and Yu (2019), The Acquisition of Cantonese Vowel Length Contrast and Vowel Rounding Contrast by South Asian Students in Hong Kong](https://ling.cuhk.edu.hk/people/peggy/ICPhS2019_MokLeungLanYu.pdf) found differences between discrimination and production and effects of phonetic environment. It supports treating hearing and producing a contrast as related but distinct tasks.

The reader-facing note deliberately makes no claim that a named nationality or language family learns Cantonese more easily. Its academic background is documented here; the research citation line is omitted from the page.

## Spelling and pronunciation

- [LSHK’s official scheme](https://lshk.org/jyutping-scheme/): origin in 1993, initials and finals, syllabic nasals, six baseline tone digits. Tone digits remain on the same line as the letters.
- [Open Cantonese initial pairs](https://opencantonese.org/books/cantonese-life-1/pronunciation-guide/pronunciation-lesson-1/3-initial-pairs): five aspiration pairs, including z/c and gw/kw.
- [Open Cantonese’s yu lesson](https://opencantonese.org/books/cantonese-life-1/pronunciation-guide/pronunciation-lesson-4/6-the-final-yu): tongue and lip position; yu is one vowel without an added initial glide.
- [Jyutping.org’s English guide](https://jyutping.org/en/learn/): vowel contrasts, initial ng and unreleased stop endings. Comparisons with English are aids, not universal sound definitions.
- [EdUHK’s tone explanation](https://corpus.eduhk.hk/cantonese/eng/chapter3/3-0.html): traditional entering-tone categories and why Jyutping uses six tone numbers.

## Resource selection

The six entries cover English-language pronunciation lessons, a Cantonese-language video, an English comparison guide, an audio chart, the official standard and a typing reference. They are described by purpose and teaching language rather than treated as interchangeable introductions.

[TypeDuck’s 52-minute video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MOsf0BcLzlc) is narrated in Cantonese. No subtitle availability is promised. Its page was accessible without signing in during review. It also appears in the [HKUST HUMA2031 Spring 2025 tutorial readings](https://huma.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/doc/Teaching/Courses/UG/Spring2025/HUMA2031.pdf); that is supporting context, not a claim of institutional endorsement of this app.

The TypeDuck cheatsheet is a multi-page spelling/typing aid using familiar Hong Kong names and places. It is not described as a complete introductory pronunciation course.
